Your memories stay on your computer.

Mnemolith has no server and no account. This page covers the Mnemolith package, the Claude Code plugin, and this website. Last updated November 3, 2026.

What Mnemolith stores

The facts your AI tools save about your project, the names of the files each fact depends on, and fingerprints of those files. They live in ~/.mnemolith on your computer, along with Mnemolith’s settings and its downloaded model.

Mnemolith isn’t meant for personal data. It stores what you or your AI choose to remember about code. Delete ~/.mnemolith, or run mnemolith uninstall, and it’s gone.

What leaves your computer

None of your memories, code, prompts or conversations. Mnemolith has no telemetry and never contacts a Mnemolith server, because there isn’t one.

It only downloads things: the package and its dependencies from PyPI, a small embedding model from Hugging Face the first time it’s needed, and, if your computer has no Java 11 or newer, a Java runtime from Adoptium. Those services see an ordinary download request, under their own privacy policies.

The engine and the dashboard listen only on 127.0.0.1, so other computers can’t reach them.

Your AI tool

Memories that Mnemolith recalls are passed to the AI tool you use, like Claude Code, Cursor or Codex, as part of your session. What that tool does with them is covered by its own privacy policy.

This website

mnemolith.dev counts page views with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and doesn’t track you across sites. It records the page, the referring site, your browser and screen size, and your country, never your IP address. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages.
